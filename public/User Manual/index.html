1. Introduction

The Renewable Lesotho RBF (Results-Based Financing) Platform is a digital system developed by the Department of Energy (DoE) in partnership with UNDP to manage, monitor, and disburse funding for renewable energy projects across Lesotho. The platform follows a results-based financing model, meaning payments are tied to verified outcomes rather than upfront inputs.

This manual describes every feature of the platform, from tendering and bidding to project implementation, field verification, KPI monitoring, and payment disbursement.

2. System Overview

The platform serves as a single source of truth for the entire RBF program lifecycle. Key capabilities include:

  • Tender Management — Create, publish, evaluate, and award tenders for renewable energy installations.
  • Vendor Pre-Qualification — Vendors register and submit pre-qualification documents for review.
  • Bidding — Qualified vendors submit stage-based proposals (Stage 1: Concept, Stage 2: Detailed).
  • Project Lifecycle Management — Track projects from setup through installation, verification, and completion.
  • Field Verification — Field verifiers (DoE officers) use GPS and checklists to validate installations.
  • Payment Claims & Multi-Tier Approval — Vendors submit milestone-based claims; RMT, TAC, and PSC review and approve.
  • KPI Monitoring — Real-time dashboards track energy output, uptime, gender impact, and inclusion targets.
  • GIS Mapping / Map View — Interactive map showing all installation sites with status indicators.
  • Audit & Compliance — Audit trail, anomaly detection, audit findings, and issues management.
  • Prospect Sync — Synchronises project data, installations, and reports with the Prospect M&E platform.

3.1 Platform Administrator (Super Admin)

The Super Admin is the system owner and top-level operator. They keep the platform running, manage every official account, and can access all modules. The Super Admin portal is the control centre for the whole system.

  • User Management — Create accounts for officials and committee members (RMT, TAC, DoE, Field Verifiers, PSC, Auditors), assign roles, reset passwords, and activate or suspend accounts.
  • Organization Registry — Manage government, international, and NGO organizations registered on the platform.
  • System Configuration — Set the national programme budget, inclusion target minimums (female 50%, vulnerable 30%, low-income 60%), uptime and anomaly thresholds, GPS duplicate radius, email/SMS notification settings, and file upload limits.
  • Payments — Full RMT payment rights on the disbursement chain (review, verify, and mark claims paid).
  • Blacklisting — Confirms blacklist cases and reviews vendor appeals.
  • System Health & Audit — Monitor system health, review audit logs, manage Prospect sync, view reports, and review notifications.

3.2 RBF Management Team (RMT) — RBF Official

The RBF Management Team is the central operator that runs the entire RBF process from tender to disbursement. Most core workflows start and finish with the RMT.

  • Tender Management — Create tenders, publish them, verify tender details, extend or shorten deadlines, issue clarifications, close bidding, and send bid reminders.
  • Pre-Qualification Review — Review vendor pre-qualification applications submitted at Stage 1 (concept) and approve, reject, or request resubmission (partial approval).
  • Financial Evaluation — Run the financial evaluation matrix (30 points) for Stage 2 bids. Financial scoring only unlocks after the technical score passes the TAC threshold.
  • Award & Contracts — Issue intent-to-award, manage the standstill/cooling-off period, review challenges, make the final award, and generate the Performance-Based Agreement (PBA) contract.
  • Project Assignment — Assign approved contracts to projects, create milestones, select districts, verification methods, and energy targets.
  • Payment Review — Review vendor payment claims, verify supporting evidence, and mark claims paid as the first step in the disbursement chain.
  • Blacklisting — Initiate blacklist cases and review them.
  • Anomaly Flag Review — Investigate system-raised anomaly flags through a controlled workflow: start an investigation, request vendor correction or evidence (vendor is notified automatically), attach evidence files, and resolve only with a complete documented dossier. See Section 6.8.
  • Portfolio & Reporting — Oversee the RBF portfolio, view reports and GIS maps, and use Audit & Oversight to follow up DoE / PSC / RMT flags (Oversight Follow-ups) and answer audit findings (Audit Findings), see Workflow D.

3.3 TAC Member (Technical Advisory Committee)

The TAC provides independent technical assessment. They score bids and endorse claims based on technical merit, but they cannot set financial scores or approve payments on their own.

  • Technical Evaluation — Score bids on the technical matrix (70 points) covering technical quality, feasibility, O&M plan, KPIs, gender, and environmental impact for both Stage 1 and Stage 2.
  • Hardware & Tier Review — Review device brand, model, and technology tier, and confirm the proposed tier does not exceed the vendor’s approved pre-qualification tier. Tier 3 or higher is treated as the preferred technical tier.
  • GPS & Field Readiness — Check that proposed sites have valid GPS coordinates and that the implementation plan is feasible before endorsing a technical score.
  • Payment Endorsement — Endorse vendor payment claims as the second step in the multi-tier disbursement chain (after RMT verification).
  • Project Technical Oversight — View projects in technical mode and participate in blacklist case reviews.

3.4 DoE Officer (Department of Energy)

DoE Officers provide the government’s regional monitoring of the programme. They visit sites, review field-verification evidence, assess project KPIs every month, comment on vendor performance and raise flags that the RBF Management Team must follow up. They do not change operational data: they cannot approve claims, edit installations, review meter uploads or resolve anomaly flags. Every record they create is their own and stays on the permanent record.

Region scope. A DoE officer sees only projects whose district or region matches the districts on their user account (or, if none are set, the region field, which may list several districts separated by commas). The Super Admin sets these in User Management. An officer with no district or region sees nothing. Financial values show as “Restricted” and documents whose name mentions “financial” or “payment” are hidden.

3.5 Field Verifier

Field Verifiers perform on-the-ground verification of installations. They work from a task list scoped to their verification zone and record evidence at each site.

  • Inspections — Conduct site visits and complete verification checklists for each assigned installation.
  • Evidence Capture — Record GPS coordinates, site photos, device serial numbers, and household counts.
  • Outcome Recording — Mark each inspection as verified, partial, or flagged based on the evidence gathered.
  • GIS & Reporting — View the GIS map and generate verification reports for their assigned area.

3.6 Project Steering Committee (PSC / UNDP Donor)

The PSC represents the donor (UNDP Lesotho and partners). It has national, read-only visibility of the whole programme and acts in three places: it gives the final approval (or rejection) of every milestone claim, it records oversight reviews on KPI results, verifications and vendor performance, and it follows up its own reviews. Procurement, contracts, audit findings and blacklisting are visible but read-only.

3.7 Auditor

The Auditor is the independent oversight role. Auditors can read every record on the platform and work through audit cases: a case links to the records under audit (tender, contract, vendor, claim, verification, project) and never changes them. Only the Auditor moves a case through its workflow; the RBF Management Team answers it. Auditors can also initiate and review blacklist cases and decide vendor appeals (with the Admin).

3.8 Vendor

Vendors are the only self-registering role. They participate in tenders and deliver the renewable energy projects.

  • Registration & Pre-Qualification — Register the organization and submit a pre-qualification application (company profile, technical & financial capacity, technology types, tier, inclusion targets, and declaration).
  • Bidding — View open tenders, submit Stage 1 concept notes, and — if shortlisted — submit Stage 2 site-specific bids with BOQ, project sites, device details, and PAYGO options. Revise bids when marked “Revision Required”.
  • Award & Contract — Track intent-to-award and award status, and sign the Performance-Based Agreement.
  • Project Setup — Configure assigned projects: field team roster, equipment plan, device brand/model/tier, meter API credentials, compliance documents, and work schedule.
  • Installation Reporting — Log installations, upload geo-tagged photos, and report device readings.
  • Payment Claims — Submit milestone-based claims and track their status through the approval chain.
  • Appeals — Appeal a blacklisting decision with a rebuttal and supporting document.

5.1 Tender Creation & Launch

Tenders are the starting point for any procurement cycle. Only users with the RBF Management Team (RMT) or Super Admin role can create, edit, publish, and manage tenders.

Accessing the Tender Module

After signing in with an RMT or Admin account, click “Tender Management” in the left sidebar to open the tender list view showing all tenders with reference number, name, status, deadline, and department. Use the filter bar to narrow results by status (Draft, Published, Evaluation, etc.), category, or department.

Creating a New Tender

Click the “Add Tender” button in the top-right corner of the tender list. This opens a multi-section creation form with steps: General Info, Timeline & Address, Technical Details, and Documents & Save.

General Information

  • Reference Number — A unique identifier (e.g., RL-2025-SHS-01).
  • Tender Name — A descriptive title.
  • Department — The issuing department.
  • Category — Technology category: SHS, Mini-Grid, ICS, SWP.
  • Funding Source — E.g., Government Budget, UNDP Grant.
  • Budget (LSL) — Total budget for this tender.

Timeline & Deadlines

  • Deadline / Last Date of Submission — Date and time after which bids are no longer accepted.
  • Last Date for Bid Security — Deadline for bid security documents.
  • Date of Opening — When bids will be opened.
  • Time for Completion — Expected project duration.

Procurement Settings

  • Procurement Method — Open Tendering, Selective Tendering, etc.
  • Application Type — Access Window or Application Window.
  • Stage Type — Defines bidding structure:
    • Pre-Qualification — Pre-qualification only.
    • Stage 1: Concept — Concept note + preliminary budget.
    • Stage 2: Detailed — Full technical + financial proposal.
    • Site-Specific — For specific installation sites.
  • Bid Security Required — Toggle whether vendors must submit bid security.
  • Bidders Schedule of Purchase — Toggle if bidders must submit a purchase schedule.

Scoring Configuration

  • Technical Weight (%) — Weight for technical score (e.g., 70).
  • Financial Weight (%) — Weight for financial score (e.g., 30). Must sum to 100.
  • Technical Threshold (%) — Minimum technical score required.
  • Cooling-Off Days — Standstill/challenge period days.

Targeting & Scope

  • Technology Types — Solar PV, Wind, Biomass, etc.
  • Target Districts — One or more districts in Lesotho.
  • Target Site Type — Household, Business, School, Clinic.
  • Approximate Installation Target — Number of expected installations.
  • Minimum Service Tier — Tier 1 through Tier 5.

Documents

  • Schedule File — Upload detailed schedule/timeline document.
  • RFP Documents — Upload full Request for Proposal package.
  • Milestone Payment Schedule — Upload milestone-linked payment plan.

Addresses & Contact Details

  • Address for Documents — Where physical documents are delivered.
  • Address for Bid Security — Where bid security instruments are sent.
  • Place for Opening — Bid opening location.
  • Contact Details — Contact person and phone/email.

  • The GPS point is outside Lesotho, or outside the project’s district (the district check runs when district boundary data is loaded).
  • The serial number is already registered on any installation in any project. Upper/lower case and extra spaces are ignored, so sn-001 and SN-001 are the same device.
  • The beneficiary ID is already registered on any installation in any project (one household, one subsidised system).

A terminated installation no longer reserves its serial number or beneficiary ID, so a replaced device or household can be registered again. The same duplicate check applies when a vendor edits an installation or resubmits a corrected one.

After a successful submission the platform creates a verification task and assigns it to a Field Verifier whose verification zone matches the installation’s district. If no verifier covers that district, the task waits unassigned until the RMT assigns one.

Important: The GPS coordinates you submit must be accurate. Field verifiers will visit the site and record their own coordinates. If the distance exceeds the threshold (default 50m), an anomaly flag is raised, which may delay milestone payments.

6.5.1 Meter Data Upload (CSV)

Once installations are submitted and meters are deployed, vendors must regularly upload smart meter readings to the platform. This data is the primary evidence used to verify that systems are operating correctly and producing energy as expected.

Why the CSV Upload is Required

  • Sustained Operation Evidence — Meter readings prove that the installed systems continue to generate energy over time, which is required for Milestone 2 (80% Implementation) and Milestone 3 (Final) payments.
  • Anomaly Detection — The platform automatically checks every uploaded reading for anomalies such as zero uptime, sudden output drops, or missing data. These checks protect the integrity of the RBF programme.
  • KPI Calculation — Aggregated meter data feeds the project KPI dashboard, which determines milestone eligibility.
  • Audit Trail — Every reading is timestamped and linked to a specific meter and project, creating an immutable record for auditors and donors.

When to Upload

  • Weekly or more frequently — Upload meter readings at least once per week to ensure no installation is flagged for missing data.
  • Within 48 hours of recording — Readings older than 48 hours may still be accepted, but consistent timely uploads prevent no_data anomaly flags.
  • After any meter reset or replacement — If a meter is replaced, upload the final reading from the old meter and the first reading from the new meter in the same batch.
  • Before submitting a milestone claim — Ensure all recent readings are uploaded so that anomaly checks do not block your payment.
Critical: If a meter sends no reading for more than 48 hours, the system automatically raises a no_data anomaly flag. While this flag does not block Milestone 2, it does block Milestone 3. Maintain a consistent upload schedule.

How to Upload (Correct Procedure)

  1. Navigate to the project detail page and click the “Device Readings” tab.
  2. Click “Upload Meter CSV” or “Bulk Upload Meter Readings”.
  3. Prepare your CSV file with the following columns (one row per meter per reporting period):
    ColumnRequired?Format / ExampleNotes
    meter_idYesText, e.g. MTR-2025-001Must match the Meter ID recorded in the installation report.
    recorded_atYesISO 8601 timestamp, e.g. 2025-08-15T08:00:00ZWhen the reading was taken. Cannot be in the future.
    kwhYesDecimal, e.g. 12.450Cumulative energy produced since installation (kWh). Should be greater than the previous reading.
    uptime_pctYesDecimal 0–100, e.g. 98.5Percentage of time the meter was operational. A value of 0 will raise a zero_uptime anomaly.
  4. Ensure the file uses UTF-8 encoding and a comma (,) delimiter.
  5. The file size limit is 10 MB. For larger datasets, split into multiple files.
  6. Click “Upload”. The system will process each row and display:
    • The number of successful readings imported.
    • Any invalid rows (with row number and reason for rejection).
    • Any anomaly flags created (with type and affected meter).
  7. Review the upload summary carefully. Invalid rows are not saved — correct them and re-upload.
Tip: Before uploading a large batch, test with a small CSV (5–10 rows) to confirm your format is correct. Once you see “0 invalid rows”, proceed with the full upload.
Tip: To avoid output_deviation anomalies, upload readings in chronological order and ensure each new kwh value is greater than the previous. Sudden jumps greater than 5% from the prior reading will be flagged.

  1. Open the project from “Projects Hub” and go to the Field Work tab.
  2. Flagged installations show the verifier’s reason under the status. Click “Fix & Resubmit”.
  3. In the correction window:

    • If the recorded location was wrong, stand at the installation and click “Capture current location”. The new point is checked against Lesotho and the district again.
    • Add photos if needed. They are added to the existing photos, not replacing them.

6.8 GPS & Anomaly Detection

The platform automatically performs checks during installation submission, field verification, and meter data uploads. When a check fails, an Anomaly Flag is created on the installation. Unresolved flags of certain types block milestone payments.

GPS Duplicate Detection

When a vendor submits an installation report, the system checks whether the submitted GPS coordinates are within the GPS Duplicate Radius (configurable, default 10m) of any other installation in the same project. If found, an anomaly flag of type duplicate_gps is raised. This flag does not block milestone payments — it is informational only and helps RMT investigate potential location fraud.

Verification Distance Check

During field verification, the system measures the distance between the vendor’s reported GPS and the verifier’s on-site GPS using the Haversine formula. If the distance exceeds the GPS Verification Max Distance (configurable, default 50m), the system forces a location_mismatch flag even if the verifier manually selected “Verified”. If the verifier flags the outcome for a different reason but distance was a factor, a verification_distance flag is created instead. Both types block Milestone 3 if unresolved.

Meter Data Anomalies (CSV Upload)

Every row in a meter CSV upload is automatically checked. The three anomaly types are:

Why Anomalies Are Raised

Anomalies are not punishments — they are automatic integrity checks built into the platform to protect the RBF programme, its funders, and its beneficiaries. Every anomaly flag exists because it answers one of four fundamental questions:

QuestionPurposeWhat Could Go Wrong Without It
“Is the system actually running?” Confirms that installed devices are operational and producing energy in the real world, not just on paper. Vendors could claim payment for non-functioning or abandoned installations, wasting programme funds and leaving beneficiaries without power.
“Is the reported output plausible?” Detects fabricated, inflated, or erroneous meter data that does not match the physical reality of the installation. Inflated kWh readings could unlock larger payouts than warranted. Erroneous data could corrupt KPI reporting to donors and government partners.
“Is data being submitted regularly?” Ensures ongoing engagement from the vendor and continuous evidence of operation. A vendor could install devices and disappear, leaving no trail of whether systems continue to serve beneficiaries.
“Is the installation where it claims to be?” Prevents location fraud and ensures installations serve the intended communities. Installations could be duplicated, moved, or placed in ineligible locations — diverting programme benefits from vulnerable populations.

These checks run automatically so that RMT and oversight bodies do not have to manually review every reading. Anomalies surface problems that need human judgement, while routine data passes through without friction.

Programme integrity principle: Results-Based Financing pays for verified outcomes, not promises. Anomaly detection ensures that every dollar disbursed is backed by evidence that the intended benefit — clean energy access for Lesotho households — is actually being delivered.

Anomaly Flag Lifecycle

  1. Auto-creation — A flag is created when a trigger condition is met. The flag includes a human-readable description (e.g., “Meter MTR-001 deviated by 12.3% from the previous uploaded reading.”).
  2. De-duplication — If an unresolved flag of the same type already exists for the same installation, the description is updated in place rather than creating a duplicate.
  3. Notification — Both the vendor and oversight users (RMT, Admin, DoE Officer) receive an in-app notification when new flags are created.
  4. GIS Impact — Any unresolved flag except duplicate_gps turns the installation RED on the GIS map, signalling risk.
  5. Milestone Blocking — zero_uptime and output_deviation block Milestone 2. All flag types block Milestone 3 if unresolved.
  6. Review & Resolution — Only RMT (or Platform Admin) reviews a flag through the Review flag modal. A flag can never be resolved in one step: it must first move to Under Investigation, and closing it requires a complete dossier (investigation notes, corrective action, decision reason, and evidence). Every decision is written to a permanent review timeline on the flag.

The Controlled Review Procedure (Step by Step)

  1. Open the case — In the project’s Anomaly Flags tab, click Review flag. The modal shows the case context (finding, installation, raised date, severity, assigned reviewer, due date), any prior review timeline, and evidence already on file. The queue’s stat cards show how many flags are open, under investigation, awaiting action, or resolved.
  2. Start the investigation — Choose Under Investigation and write factual investigation notes: what you checked (meter readings, KPI data, GPS records, photos, verification history) and what you found. The requirements checklist ticks green as each mandatory item is completed.
  3. Involve the vendor where needed — If the vendor must act, choose Correction Requested (requires notes, a corrective action, and a due date) or Awaiting Evidence (due date defaults to 14 days). In both cases the platform notifies the vendor automatically so they know what is expected and by when. The vendor answers with the Respond button, which returns the flag to Under Investigation for you.
  4. Close with a complete dossier — To resolve (or dismiss as false positive) you must provide investigation notes, corrective action taken, a decision reason, and an evidence reference or uploaded evidence files. The save button names your action (for example Start investigation, Request correction, or Resolve flag) so it is always clear what will be recorded.
  5. Escalate or reopen when appropriate — Use Escalated for issues beyond RMT authority (escalation requires notes and a reason). A resolved or false-positive flag can be Reopened if the decision is challenged or new evidence appears; investigation then restarts through the same controlled path.

Evidence Files and the Review Timeline

  • Attach evidence files — The review modal accepts jpg, png, webp, or pdf files up to 5 MB each. Files are stored against the flag, listed under Evidence on file, and downloadable by reviewers.
  • Permanent timeline — Every review action writes a permanent entry recording who acted, when, the previous and new states, and the notes captured at that moment. Timeline entries cannot be edited or deleted afterwards, giving auditors a complete decision trail.
  • KPI impact — Each review action refreshes project KPIs immediately, so Milestone 2/3 eligibility always reflects the current flag states. Unresolved blocking flags keep the relevant milestone conditions unmet.
No shortcuts: The legacy one-click resolution path has been removed. Any attempt to resolve a flag that is not under investigation is rejected by the platform, and all decisions require the same documented dossier regardless of who performs them.

Vendor Responses and Due Dates

When the RMT moves a flag to Correction Requested or Awaiting Evidence, the next move is the vendor’s:

  1. The vendor opens the project’s Anomaly Flags tab. The flag shows a purple “Respond” button.
  2. The response window shows what the RMT asked for (corrective action and notes) and the due date.
  3. The vendor writes what they did or what the evidence shows (at least 10 characters) and can attach jpg, png, webp or pdf files up to 5 MB each.
  4. Clicking “Send to RMT” moves the flag back to Under Investigation, adds the files to the flag’s evidence, writes a timeline entry by the vendor, and notifies the assigned RMT reviewer (or all RMT and Admin users if nobody is assigned).
  5. The RMT sees the vendor’s response in a purple box in the Review flag window and decides the next step.

The vendor can respond only while the flag is in one of those two states, and only on its own projects.

6.9 RMT: Managing Verification Outcomes

After a verification report is submitted:

  • Confirm Verified — Override flags if the installation is legitimate.
  • Request Re-Verification — A new verification round for the same installation. In the current screens this is done by the DoE Officer from Field Verification (section 8.3); the RMT sees the request in Oversight Follow-ups, and it closes automatically when the verifier submits the new round.

7.11 Partial Payments, Returns & Carry-Overs

Results-based financing pays for what is verified. When a vendor claims a milestone before every required unit is verified, the RMT has two options besides full approval.

How delivery is measured

Each milestone requires a cumulative share of the project’s target installations. A milestone’s own units are the difference from the previous milestone. Example for a project with a target of 100 installations:

MilestoneRequired (cumulative)Own unitsDelivered % if 60 are verified
M280% = 80 verified8060 / 80 = 75%
M3100% = 100 verified20 + any units carried over from M2—

Approve partially (RMT)

  • Allowed only when the delivered share is at least the Partial Payment Minimum (Super Admin setting, default 70%) and below 100%.
  • Not allowed on the final milestone: there is no later milestone to carry the shortfall to.
  • The RMT may enter a lower assessed percentage than the platform calculated (never a higher one).
  • The RMT must list each missing item: a description, the required action and, optionally, the installation IDs.
  • The approved amount = claim amount × assessed %. In the example above, a M 2,000 claim at 75% approves M 1,500; the shortfall is M 500 (20 units).

Carry-overs

Carry-over stateWhen
PendingCreated when a partially-approved claim is marked paid. The next milestone card shows the pending carry-in, and the next claim’s amount automatically includes it (M3 claim = M3 amount + M 500).
SettledThe claim that included the carry-over is paid.
ForfeitedThe RMT closes the project in a milestone completion review before the shortfall is delivered.

The carried units are added to the next milestone’s required delivery: in the example, the M3 claim must show 20 + 20 = 40 newly verified units for a full approval.

Return for completion

  1. The RMT chooses Return for completion, lists the missing items, and clicks “Return to Vendor”.
  2. The claim becomes Returned for Completion; the vendor is notified.
  3. The vendor opens the claim from the project’s Payments tab. The “Payment Details” window lists the deficiencies under “Complete before resubmitting”.
  4. After completing the items (for example, getting the remaining installations verified), the vendor adds a note and clicks “Resubmit Claim”.
  5. The same claim returns to Submitted and the RMT reviews it again. The resubmission count is recorded.

The “Anomaly Report” section (Auditor sidebar) shows all system-generated anomaly flags. The project-level Anomaly Flags tab (RMT) shows flags per project. The system automatically detects:

  • GPS coordinate duplicates — flagged as duplicate_gps; not payment-blocking.
  • Verification distance threshold violations — flagged as location_mismatch / verification_distance; blocks M3 if unresolved.
  • Zero uptime reported — flagged as zero_uptime; blocks M2 and M3.
  • Output deviation > 5% from previous reading — flagged as output_deviation; blocks M2 and M3.
  • No data uploaded in 48 hours — flagged as no_data; blocks M3 only.
  • Other data integrity issues surfaced from the audit pipeline.

See section 6.8 for the full anomaly lifecycle and resolution rules.

12.3 Audit Cases and Findings

Formal audit work is recorded as audit cases (Auditor sidebar → Audit Cases, or Compliance Reviews for compliance reviews). A case links to the records under audit, collects evidence, records a finding with a risk level (Observation, Minor, Major, Critical) and a corrective action, and asks the RBF Management Team for a response. The RMT answers under Audit & Oversight → Audit Findings; the PSC can read the case from that point under Audit Findings. The full procedure is in section 8.10.

12.4 Oversight Reviews and Flags

The former “Raise Concern” and “Issues & Findings” screens have been replaced by oversight reviews. DoE officers, the PSC and the RMT record reviews (Compliant, Delayed, Flagged or Comment) on verifications, KPI results, vendors, projects and disbursements. Flagged and Delayed reviews open a follow-up that the RMT tracks under Audit & Oversight → Oversight Follow-ups. See sections 8.7–8.9.

A Flagged oversight review recorded with “Hold milestone payments”, an audit case on a claim, a major or critical audit exception, and a DoE site visit that found a system not working all hold the project’s milestone payments until they are resolved. See 7.12 for the exact rules.

15. Blacklisting

The blacklisting module protects the integrity of the Renewable Lesotho RBF Programme. It is used to suspend or exclude vendors who violate programme rules, engage in fraud, or fail to deliver on their contractual obligations. This section explains why and how vendors are blacklisted, what the consequences are, and how a vendor can apply to be removed from the blacklist.

15.1 Why Vendors Get Blacklisted

Blacklisting is not a first-line enforcement tool. It is reserved for serious or repeated violations that compromise the programme’s integrity, its beneficiaries, or its funders. The platform supports two enforcement tiers before blacklisting is considered:

SeverityStatusEffectReversible?
Tier 1 — Minor issues Active Normal operations. Vendor can bid, install, verify, and claim. N/A
Tier 2 — Under review / compliance issue Suspended Field verification and new bid submissions are paused. Vendor cannot sign new contracts but can still respond to existing case communications. Yes — RMT can lift the suspension once the issue is resolved.
Tier 3 — Serious or repeated violation Blacklisted Vendor is excluded from bidding on tenders, signing contracts, receiving payments, and all active projects are halted. Only through a successful appeal or natural expiry of a temporary blacklist.

A blacklist case is initiated when any of the following trigger conditions are observed:

A. Fraud or Misrepresentation

  • Submitting fabricated installation reports, beneficiary data, or meter readings.
  • Falsifying GPS coordinates (e.g., placing multiple installations at the same GPS point to inflate completion numbers — the duplicate_gps anomaly flag is one signal).
  • Submitting claims for work that was not performed, or for systems that are not operational.
  • Falsifying pre-qualification documents (company registration, tax clearance, certifications).

B. Contractual Non-Performance

  • Failure to deliver installations within the agreed timeline without valid justification.
  • Abandoning an awarded project mid-implementation.
  • Refusing to cooperate with field verification, anomaly resolution, or RMT requests for information.

C. Quality or Safety Failures

  • Installing substandard or counterfeit equipment that does not meet the agreed technical specifications.
  • Repeated unresolved anomaly flags indicating systematic hardware or data issues (e.g., persistent zero_uptime or output_deviation flags across many meters).
  • Endangering beneficiary safety through improper installation practices.

D. Compliance Violations

  • Breach of the code of conduct (e.g., soliciting bribes, discriminatory beneficiary selection).
  • Violation of procurement rules (collusion with other bidders, bid rigging).
  • Criminal conviction related to fraud, corruption, or similar offences.
Important: Single minor compliance issues do not result in blacklisting. They are first handled through the anomaly flag system, RMT requests for clarification, or temporary suspension. Blacklisting is reserved for cases where lesser measures have failed or where the violation is severe enough to warrant immediate exclusion.

15.2 How a Blacklist Case Is Opened and Processed

The platform implements a five-step cross-role approval chain to ensure fairness:

  1. Initiation (RMT or Auditor) — An authorised user opens a blacklist case against a vendor. The case must include a clear reason, severity classification (temporary or permanent), and a justification document (evidence, audit findings, or incident report).
  2. Notice & Cooling-Off — The vendor receives an in-app and email notification of the case, along with a cooling-off period during which they may submit a rebuttal and supporting documents.
  3. Review (TAC, DoE Officer, or Auditor) — A different oversight role independently reviews the case, the evidence, and the vendor’s rebuttal. They record review notes that become part of the audit trail.
  4. Confirmation (Admin or DoE Officer) — A final decision-maker confirms or rejects the blacklisting. If confirmed, the status becomes Suspended (temporary with expiry) or Blacklisted (permanent).
  5. Appeal (Vendor) — The vendor may submit a formal appeal at any point after confirmation, with a written rebuttal and supporting documents.
  6. Appeal Resolution (Auditor or Admin) — The appeal is reviewed and resolved as either Upheld (blacklisting stands), Reinstated (vendor returned to Active status), or Requalified (vendor must reapply for pre-qualification).

15.3 Consequences of Being Blacklisted

When a vendor status changes to Blacklisted, the following restrictions take effect immediately:

  • No new bids — The vendor cannot view, submit, or revise bids on any active tender. Existing draft bids are locked.
  • No new contracts — The vendor cannot sign new tender contracts or be assigned new projects.
  • Payment holds — All pending payment claims are frozen. Approved claims that have not yet been disbursed are placed on hold pending resolution of the case.
  • Projects halted — All active projects the vendor is implementing are transitioned to Halted status. No new installations, verifications, or meter readings can be submitted.
  • Visibility removed — The vendor does not appear in public tender opportunity lists or in RMT dropdowns for new project assignment.
  • Audit trail — Every action taken on the case (initiation, reviews, vendor rebuttals, appeal, resolution) is logged with timestamps, actor identity, and attached documents.
Important: A temporary blacklist automatically lifts when its expiry date passes, provided no further violations have been recorded. A permanent blacklisting does not expire on its own — it can only be removed through a successful appeal or formal programme decision.

15.4 How a Vendor Can Apply for Removal (Appeal Process)

If you are a vendor whose status has been changed to Suspended or Blacklisted, you have the right to appeal. The platform’s appeal process is designed to be fair, transparent, and well-documented.

Step 1 — Review the Case Details

  1. Log in to the platform with your vendor credentials (access is retained in read-only mode for the duration of the case).
  2. Navigate to “My Profile” → “Audit Trail” tab.
  3. Open the blacklist case entry to view the reason, severity, attached evidence, and the cooling-off deadline.
  4. If you believe the case is incorrect, gather all supporting documents (photos, correspondence, corrected data, witness statements) before submitting your appeal.

Step 2 — Submit a Rebuttal (During Cooling-Off)

During the cooling-off period (set when the case is initiated), you may submit a rebuttal directly to the RMT:

  1. Open the case from your profile.
  2. Click “Submit Rebuttal”.
  3. Provide a clear written explanation of why the case should not proceed.
  4. Upload any supporting documents.
  5. Click “Submit”. The RMT will see your rebuttal alongside the original case when making their review notes.

Step 3 — File a Formal Appeal (After Confirmation)

If the case is confirmed and your status changes to Blacklisted, you may file a formal appeal:

  1. Navigate to “Blacklisting” in the sidebar.
  2. Find your case in the list and click “File Appeal”.
  3. Complete the appeal form with:
    • Appeal grounds — The specific reason you believe the blacklisting is incorrect (e.g., new evidence, procedural error, mistaken identity, data correction).
    • Rebuttal narrative — A detailed explanation addressing each point in the original case.
    • Supporting documents — Upload files that substantiate your appeal (photos, corrected meter data, correspondence, third-party verification reports).
  4. Click “Submit Appeal”. The Auditor and Admin are notified immediately.

Step 4 — Wait for Appeal Resolution

The Auditor or Admin will review your appeal and attached documents. They may:

  • Request additional information — You will receive a notification and can respond with more evidence.
  • Uphold the blacklisting — The decision stands. You may not file a second appeal on the same case.
  • Reinstate you — Your status returns to Active. All active projects resume their previous state, payment holds are released, and you can bid on new tenders again.
  • Requalify you — Your account is reinstated, but you must resubmit pre-qualification documents before bidding on new tenders.
Tip for vendors: Submit your appeal as soon as possible after confirmation. A well-prepared appeal with clear evidence is more likely to succeed than a late, incomplete one. If you need time to gather documents, file a preliminary appeal first with the information you have and add more documents as they become available.

Step 5 — Automatic Reinstatement (Temporary Blacklists Only)

If your blacklisting was marked temporary with an expiry date, no action is required on your part. On the expiry date:

  1. Your status automatically returns to Active.
  2. Your projects automatically resume from their last saved state.
  3. Payment holds are released.
  4. You regain access to bid on new tenders.

If a new violation occurs during or after a temporary blacklist, the case may be escalated to permanent blacklisting or a longer suspension period.

15.5 Best Practices for Vendors

  • Respond promptly to RMT communications. Ignoring anomaly flags or RMT requests is itself a trigger for escalation.
  • Keep documentation organised. Photos, signed beneficiary forms, meter serial labels, and installation records are your first line of defence if a case is opened.
  • Upload meter readings on schedule. Consistent weekly uploads prevent no_data flags, which over time can contribute to a compliance review.
  • Use accurate GPS at submission time. Stand at the actual installation site. A duplicate_gps or location_mismatch flag can be investigated further if recurring.
  • Train field staff on the beneficiary verification process. Errors in household classification (female-headed, vulnerable, low-income) can be flagged during KPI review and trigger audits.
  • Maintain open communication with RMT. If you anticipate delays, report them early. Proactive communication is treated more favourably than silence.

17. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Why can't I see any tenders?

Ensure your vendor account is pre-qualified and approved. Only approved vendors can see published tenders in “Published Tenders”. Also check that your account status is “Active” and not “Suspended” or “Blacklisted.”

Q: My bid was rejected. Can I resubmit?

If the RMT requests revisions, you can resubmit. Otherwise, you may need to wait for the next tender cycle.

Q: How do I update my company information?

Navigate to “My Profile” in the sidebar. The profile has tabs: Company Profile, Documents, Bids & Tenders, Projects, Performance, Payments, and Audit Trail. Click “Edit Profile” to update contact details, bank information, and uploaded documents. Changes to pre-qualification status require re-review by the RMT.

Q: When will I receive payment after submitting a claim?

Payment processing time depends on the multi-tier approval workflow (RMT → TAC → PSC). Each tier must approve before disbursement is initiated. Check the claim status from “Payment Claims” in the sidebar or the Payments tab within your project.

Q: What does the “Flagged” status mean on my installation?

Flagged installations require attention. A flag is raised when GPS coordinates are duplicated, verification distance exceeds thresholds, or discrepancies are found. The RMT will review and either resolve or escalate the flag.

Q: How do I use the GIS map?

The GIS map is available from the sidebar (“GIS Mapping” for RMT, “GIS Map” for Auditor, “Map View” for Field Verifier). It loads all installation locations in Lesotho. Use mouse scroll to zoom, click markers to view details.

Q: Can I download reports?

Yes, the “Reports” section (or “Briefings & Reports” for PSC) allows downloading in CSV, PDF, and Excel formats. Available reports depend on your role and permissions.

Q: My account was blacklisted. What can I do?

You have the right to appeal. Log in (access remains in read-only mode), open the case from “My Profile” → “Audit Trail”, and either submit a rebuttal during the cooling-off period or file a formal appeal after confirmation. Include a clear written explanation and any supporting documents (corrected meter data, photos, correspondence). The Auditor or Admin will review your appeal and may reinstate you, uphold the blacklisting, or require re-qualification. Temporary blacklists also lift automatically on their expiry date. See section 15 for the full process.

Q: What is the difference between “Suspended” and “Blacklisted”?

Suspended is a temporary hold used during compliance reviews — the vendor pauses field verification and new bids but can still respond to communications and the suspension can be lifted. Blacklisted is a formal exclusion from the programme: no bidding, no payments, all projects halted. A blacklist can be temporary (with an expiry date) or permanent. See section 15.1 for the full severity tier comparison.

Q: Why is my claim stuck even though all approvers agreed?

It is probably on hold because of an open finding on the project: a high or critical anomaly, an escalated anomaly, an oversight flag that holds payment, an audit, or a DoE site visit that found a system not working. Officials see a red “Payment on hold” panel listing each one. Vendors should check the Anomaly Flags tab and answer any Respond request. See 7.12.

Q: Why was I paid less than I claimed?

The RMT approved the claim partially because not every required installation was verified. The unpaid shortfall carries over and is added to your next milestone claim once the missing units are delivered and verified. See 7.11.

Q: My installation was flagged. Do I have to submit a new one?

No. Use “Fix & Resubmit” on the Field Work tab to correct it and send it back for another verification visit (up to 3 times). See 6.5.2.

Q: Why can’t I register a device or household?

Its serial number or beneficiary ID is already registered on another installation, in this or any other project. Only a terminated installation releases them. See 6.5.

Q: Why can’t I change the claim amount?

The amount is fixed by the milestone, plus any carry-over from an earlier partial payment. See 7.3.

18.13 Known Gaps (Not Yet Built)

These behaviours are known and planned. Note them if you meet them, but they are not new defects:

  • Reinstating a blacklisted or suspended vendor does not release Held/Audit claims, restore paused installations or reactivate halted projects.
  • Field verifier GPS can be typed manually; there is no sampling re-verification at claim time.
  • Meter data arrives by CSV only; the live meter API pull is not connected.
  • No warranty / O&M period, retention, penalties or beneficiary grievance channel.
  • Projects are fixed to three milestones; contract amendments and ceilings are not modelled.
  • Oversight flags recorded before version 1.1 do not hold payment, even if they concern a claim.
  • RMT and Admin have no screen to acknowledge a verification or request re-verification; only the DoE officer can do it from Field Verification.
  • Only the DoE officer who logged a site visit can close its follow-up, and therefore release a “system not working” payment hold. RMT cannot edit visits.
  • Edits do not re-notify: changing a site visit’s follow-up to Open, or a KPI review to At Risk, sends no notification; only new records do.
  • Super Admin has no audit-case screen (Admin can respond through the API and receives audit notifications).
  • “Flag for Audit” on an RMT project records an oversight flag, not an audit case.
  • The KPI gate on PSC approval is checked in the browser only; the server checks payment holds and vendor status.
  • DoE blacklist recommendations check the single region field, so an officer whose districts are stored only as a list may be refused.
