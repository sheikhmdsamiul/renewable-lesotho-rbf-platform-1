1. Introduction

The Renewable Lesotho RBF (Results-Based Financing) Platform is a digital system developed by the Department of Energy (DoE) in partnership with UNDP to manage, monitor, and disburse funding for renewable energy projects across Lesotho. The platform follows a results-based financing model, meaning payments are tied to verified outcomes rather than upfront inputs.

This manual describes every feature of the platform, from tendering and bidding to project implementation, field verification, KPI monitoring, and payment disbursement.

2. System Overview

The platform serves as a single source of truth for the entire RBF program lifecycle. Key capabilities include:

  • Tender Management — Create, publish, evaluate, and award tenders for renewable energy installations.
  • Vendor Pre-Qualification — Vendors register and submit pre-qualification documents for review.
  • Bidding — Qualified vendors submit stage-based proposals (Stage 1: Concept, Stage 2: Detailed).
  • Project Lifecycle Management — Track projects from setup through installation, verification, and completion.
  • Field Verification — Field verifiers (DoE officers) use GPS and checklists to validate installations.
  • Payment Claims & Multi-Tier Approval — Vendors submit milestone-based claims; RMT, TAC, and PSC review and approve.
  • KPI Monitoring — Real-time dashboards track energy output, uptime, gender impact, and inclusion targets.
  • GIS Mapping / Map View — Interactive map showing all installation sites with status indicators.
  • Audit & Compliance — Audit trail, anomaly detection, audit findings, and issues management.
  • Prospect Sync — Synchronises project data, installations, and reports with the Prospect M&E platform.

5. Workflow A — Full RBF Lifecycle

This section describes the complete end-to-end RBF lifecycle, from tender creation through vendor registration, pre-qualification, bidding, evaluation, award, contract signing, project creation, and final closure. Each step involves specific user roles and actions.

5.1 Tender Creation & Launch

Tenders are the starting point for any procurement cycle. Only users with the RBF Management Team (RMT) or Super Admin role can create, edit, publish, and manage tenders.

Accessing the Tender Module

After signing in with an RMT or Admin account, click “Tender Management” in the left sidebar to open the tender list view showing all tenders with reference number, name, status, deadline, and department. Use the filter bar to narrow results by status (Draft, Published, Evaluation, etc.), category, or department.

Creating a New Tender

Click the “Add Tender” button in the top-right corner of the tender list. This opens a multi-section creation form with steps: General Info, Timeline & Address, Technical Details, and Documents & Save.

General Information

  • Reference Number — A unique identifier (e.g., RL-2025-SHS-01).
  • Tender Name — A descriptive title.
  • Department — The issuing department.
  • Category — Technology category: SHS, Mini-Grid, ICS, SWP.
  • Funding Source — E.g., Government Budget, UNDP Grant.
  • Budget (LSL) — Total budget for this tender.

Timeline & Deadlines

  • Deadline / Last Date of Submission — Date and time after which bids are no longer accepted.
  • Last Date for Bid Security — Deadline for bid security documents.
  • Date of Opening — When bids will be opened.
  • Time for Completion — Expected project duration.

Procurement Settings

  • Procurement Method — Open Tendering, Selective Tendering, etc.
  • Application Type — Access Window or Application Window.
  • Stage Type — Defines bidding structure:
    • Pre-Qualification — Pre-qualification only.
    • Stage 1: Concept — Concept note + preliminary budget.
    • Stage 2: Detailed — Full technical + financial proposal.
    • Site-Specific — For specific installation sites.
  • Bid Security Required — Toggle whether vendors must submit bid security.
  • Bidders Schedule of Purchase — Toggle if bidders must submit a purchase schedule.

Scoring Configuration

  • Technical Weight (%) — Weight for technical score (e.g., 70).
  • Financial Weight (%) — Weight for financial score (e.g., 30). Must sum to 100.
  • Technical Threshold (%) — Minimum technical score required.
  • Cooling-Off Days — Standstill/challenge period days.

Targeting & Scope

  • Technology Types — Solar PV, Wind, Biomass, etc.
  • Target Districts — One or more districts in Lesotho.
  • Target Site Type — Household, Business, School, Clinic.
  • Approximate Installation Target — Number of expected installations.
  • Minimum Service Tier — Tier 1 through Tier 5.

Documents

  • Schedule File — Upload detailed schedule/timeline document.
  • RFP Documents — Upload full Request for Proposal package.
  • Milestone Payment Schedule — Upload milestone-linked payment plan.

Addresses & Contact Details

  • Address for Documents — Where physical documents are delivered.
  • Address for Bid Security — Where bid security instruments are sent.
  • Place for Opening — Bid opening location.
  • Contact Details — Contact person and phone/email.
  • Pre-Tender Meeting Info — Pre-bid meeting details.

Eligibility & Currency

  • Bidders Eligibility — Who is eligible (e.g., “Pre-qualified vendors only”).
  • Bidding Currency — LSL, USD, etc.
  • Instructions — Additional instructions for bidders.
Tip: You can save the tender as Draft at any point and return later. Draft tenders are only visible to RMT and Admin. When complete, use the Publish button to make the tender visible to pre-qualified vendors.

Starting Your Application

  2. Click “Pre-Qualification” to open the application form.
  3. Complete each section using “Next Step” to proceed, then “Submit Pre-Qualification” at the end.

  • Tax ID / VAT Number — Tax registration number.

  • Contact Number — Primary phone number.

  • Number of Prior Projects — Similar past projects.

Gender & Inclusion Commitments

5.4 Published Tenders & Bidding

Pre-qualified vendors browse available tenders from “Published Tenders” in the sidebar. Tenders with a two-stage process split bidding into two distinct phases: a Stage 1 Concept Proposal followed by a Stage 2 Detailed Proposal. Stage 2 is only accessible after RMT shortlists the Stage 1 submission.

Stage 1: Submitting a Concept Proposal

  1. Navigate to “Published Tenders” and click a tender card.
  2. Click “Submit Proposal” to start a Stage 1 draft.
  3. Complete the form across these sections:
    • Financial — Concept-stage budget: bid amount and subsidy requested.
    • Geography — Target district, site name, primary beneficiary type, and estimated household count. GPS is optional at this stage.
    • Inclusion — Commitments for female-headed, vulnerable, and low-income beneficiary percentages.
    • Narrative — Concept note / implementation strategy describing project understanding and approach (minimum 200 characters).
  4. Use “Save Draft” to save progress. When complete, click “Submit Final Proposal”.
Important: Once a Stage 1 bid is submitted, you cannot edit it. If the RMT requires changes, they will set the status to Revision Required, allowing you to resubmit. The deadline still applies.

Stage 1 Review & Shortlisting

After submission, RMT reviews Stage 1 proposals from “Financial Evaluation” in the sidebar. The RMT opens “Stage 1 Evaluation” and chooses either “Shortlist” (bid accepted, Stage 2 unlocked) or “Reject” (bid denied with a reason). Only shortlisted vendors can proceed to Stage 2.

Stage 2: Submitting a Detailed Proposal

When a Stage 1 bid is shortlisted, the vendor sees a Stage 2 unlocked badge and a “Continue Stage 2” button. A Stage 2 draft is auto-created:

  1. Click “Continue Stage 2” to open the detailed proposal form.
  2. Complete the form across these sections:
    • Financial — Upload the Bill of Quantities (BOQ) with itemised components, quantities, and prices.
    • Technical — Hardware specifications: technology type, device brand, model, and technology tier (cannot exceed approved pre-qualification tier).
    • Geography — Exact village list with mandatory GPS coordinates, household count, and access details for field verification.
    • Inclusion — Binding inclusion targets plus gender action plan upload.
    • Narrative — O&M strategy summary, implementation notes, and PAYGO details (for SHS tenders).
    • Documents — Upload required files: tender security, gender action plan, and other supporting documents.
  3. Use “Save Draft” to save progress. When complete, click “Submit Final Proposal”.

Tracking Your Bid

Monitor bid statuses from “My Bids” in the sidebar:

  • Stage 1 path: Draft → Submitted → Shortlisted (Accepted) / Rejected / Revision Required
  • Stage 2 path: Draft → Submitted → Under Review → Awarded / Not Awarded / Revision Required

During standstill, click “File Challenge” to dispute an award.

5.5 Evaluation & Scoring

Evaluation is split between TAC (technical) and RMT (financial).

TAC: Technical Evaluation

TAC members access the scoring screen from “Evaluations” in the sidebar. The page header reads “TAC Stage 2 Technical Evaluation”. TAC evaluates Stage 2 (Detailed Proposal) submissions only:

  1. Click “Start Technical Score” on a bid to open the “Technical Evaluation” form.
  2. Enter scores in the Technical Scoring Matrix:
    • Technical design quality (max 20)
    • Technology tier (max 15)
    • O&M strategy (max 10)
    • Implementation plan (max 10)
    • Gender/inclusion approach (max 10)
    • Local capacity (max 5)
    • Technical Total auto-calculated (/70)
  3. Add justification comments and click “Save Score”.
Note: TAC sees a blue info box: “TAC reviews technical feasibility and readiness only. Financial details do not drive the technical endorsement decision on this screen.”

RMT: Financial Evaluation

RMT members access from “Financial Evaluation” in the sidebar. The page header reads “RMT Evaluation Screens”. RMT handles two stages:

  1. Stage 1 Review — Click “Review” to open “Stage 1 Evaluation”. Choose “Shortlist” or “Reject”. Shortlisting auto-creates a Stage 2 draft for the vendor, who will then see a “Continue Stage 2” button.
  2. Stage 2 Financial Scoring — Click “Start Financial Score” and enter scores in RMT Financial Scoring:
    • Value for money (max 10)
    • Subsidy reasonableness (max 10)
    • BOQ accuracy (max 5)
    • Co-financing contribution (max 5)
    • Financial Total auto-calculated (/30)
  3. Add evaluation notes and click “Save Score”.

The system combines Technical (/70) and Financial (/30) into a weighted total (/100). Bids below the configured technical threshold are disqualified.

5.6 Award Process

After all bids are evaluated, the RMT follows these steps:

  1. Open Award Panel — From the tender detail page, click “Award” or “Manage Award”. The panel shows the Comparative Ranking Table with all bids sorted by combined score.
  2. Select Winner — Review rankings. The system recommends the highest-scoring bid. RMT can override with a reason.
  3. Issue Intent to Award — Click “Issue Intent to Award”. This triggers Standstill status and starts the cooling-off period.
  4. Manage Cooling-Off — Use “Adjust Cooling-Off” to extend or shorten, “Pause Award” to pause, or “Revoke Intent” to cancel.
  5. Confirm Final Award — After the period expires without challenges, click “Confirm Final Award” to generate the Performance-Based Agreement (PBA).

5.7 Standstill, Challenges & Disputes

After the Intent to Award is issued, the tender enters Standstill (cooling-off). During this window, any bidder who submitted a bid may file a challenge:

  • Filing a Challenge — The challenging vendor clicks “File Challenge” on the tender detail page, provides grounds, and submits. Status changes to Disputed.
  • RMT Reviews — Actions available:
    • Dismiss — Challenge deemed invalid; award proceeds.
    • Upheld — Challenge is valid. Award revoked, tender returns to Evaluation.
  • Resolved status returns to Standstill (dismissed) or Evaluation (upheld).

5.8 Contracts & Project Creation

PBA Generation

Clicking “Confirm Final Award” immediately triggers the system to generate a Performance-Based Agreement (PBA) — the legally binding contract between the RBF authority and the awarded vendor. The PBA is a single PDF composed of:

  • Main Agreement — Templated legal text with parties, scope, term, payment structure, and signature blocks.
  • Annex A — Results Framework — Derived from the vendor’s Gender Action Plan.
  • Annex B — Implementation Schedule — From the vendor’s implementation plan.
  • Annex C — Payment Terms — From the financial proposal and BOQ.
  • Annex D — Reporting Formats — From the reporting templates and milestone payment schedule.
  • Annex E — Technical Standards — From the technical proposal.

The PBA is stored on the platform and becomes available in the Contracting section for both the vendor and RMT.

Vendor Signs the Contract

Once the PBA is generated, the vendor receives a notification to review and sign:

  1. Navigate to the awarded tender and open the Contracting tab.
  2. Click “Download generated Performance-Based Agreement” to review the full PBA PDF including all annexes.
  3. Review Annexes A–E (locked from the bid documents).
  4. Sign the document (digital signature or wet signature scanned to PDF).
  5. Upload the signed PDF using the Upload Signed Contract field and click “Submit”.

After submission, the contract status changes to Submitted and the RMT is notified to review the signed document.

RMT Approves & Assigns Project

The RMT opens the signed contract from the tender detail page and clicks “Save & Assign Project”. This performs two actions:

  1. Approve Contract — Status changes to Approved with timestamp and reviewer record.
  2. Create Project — The system creates a Project with three milestones:
    • M1 — Mobilization (20%) — Status: Pending (can be claimed after project setup).
    • M2 — 80% Implementation (50%) — Status: Locked.
    • M3 — Final (30%) — Status: Locked.

The project reference follows the format PRJ-{tender-ref}-{vendor-id} and appears in both the vendor’s and RMT’s project lists with Setup Pending status. The RMT can also configure the project assignment form with districts, technology, duration, and installation targets before finalising.

5.9 Archival, Audit & Closure

When a tender reaches Closed status, it becomes view-only with no further actions available. All actions throughout the lifecycle are logged in the audit trail. When the final milestone of a project is paid, the project automatically closes. Archived tenders and projects remain accessible via search and filter.

  • Meter API Token — Authentication token for meter API.

Verification Method

Setup Checklist

Tip: You can save as draft and return later. Once all sections are complete, click “Submit Project Setup”. The RMT will be notified and can request changes if needed.

6.4 RMT Setup Review

When a vendor submits project setup, review each section. Verify the team roster, equipment plan, work schedule, compliance documents, and insurance. Use “Test Connection” to verify the meter API. To send the setup back with comments, use “Request Change”. The project advances on vendor submission.

6.5 Installation Phase (Vendor)

Once setup is approved and the project status is Installation, navigate to the “Field Work” tab and click “Submit Installation”. Fill in the installation details:

  • Serial Number — Unique device serial number.
  • Beneficiary ID / Name — Beneficiary identifier and full name.
  • Household Type — Female-headed, Vulnerable, Low-income, Standard.
  • GPS Coordinates — Latitude/longitude of the installation site.
  • Meter ID — Smart meter identifier (if applicable).
  • kWh Reading — Initial meter reading (if applicable).

Upload evidence photos and submit. The report is recorded and queued for field verification.

Important: The GPS coordinates you submit must be accurate. Field verifiers will visit the site and record their own coordinates. If the distance exceeds the threshold (default 50m), an anomaly flag is raised, which may delay milestone payments.

6.6 Milestones & Progress

Each project is assigned a Milestone Plan during project creation. The standard plan has three milestones based on the PBA disbursement schedule:

MilestoneTrigger ConditionPayout %

Milestone States

  • Locked — Preceding conditions not met. No action possible.
  • Pending — Conditions met; RMT verifying readiness.
  • Claimable — Ready for payment claim submission.
  • Claimed — Claim submitted and in approval workflow.
  • Paid — Disbursement completed successfully.

6.7 Field Verification

Field verification is the process of physically visiting installation sites to confirm that devices are installed, operational, and match the vendor’s submitted reports. This is a critical control mechanism in the RBF model.

Accessing Tasks (Field Verifier)

  1. Sign in with your Field Verifier account.
  2. Click “Verifications” in the sidebar to see assigned installations.
  3. View all installation locations on “Map View” from the sidebar.

Conducting a Field Visit

  1. Travel to the site — Navigate to the GPS coordinates provided by the vendor.
  2. Locate the installation — Verify the serial number matches the submitted report.
  3. Record your GPS position — Click “Use Current Location” (or enter manually).
  4. Inspect the installation — Check presence, operation, installation quality.
  5. Complete the checklist — Answer verification questions.
  6. Click “Next Step” to navigate the form, then “Submit Verification”.

Verification Outcomes

OutcomeStatusNext Step
Confirmed and matches reportVerifiedCounted toward milestone progress.
Minor discrepanciesPartialRMT reviews; may request clarification.
Significant issuesFlaggedAnomaly flag raised; RMT investigates.

6.8 GPS & Anomaly Detection

The platform automatically performs checks during verification:

GPS Duplicate Detection

Checks whether vendor-submitted GPS coordinates are within the GPS Duplicate Radius (configurable, default 10m) of any other installation by the same vendor. If found, an anomaly flag is raised with type “GPS Location Fraud”.

Verification Distance Check

Measures distance between vendor coordinates and verifier coordinates. If it exceeds GPS Verification Max Distance (configurable, default 50m), the verification is flagged as anomalous.

Anomaly Flags

Anomaly flags are auto-created with flag type, description, status (Resolved/Unresolved), and linked installation. RMT reviews and resolves or escalates flags. Unresolved flags can block milestone progression and payment claims.

6.9 RMT: Managing Verification Outcomes

After a verification report is submitted:

  • Confirm Verified — Override flags if the installation is legitimate.
  • Request Re-Verification — Assign a different verifier for a second visit.
  • Initiate Investigation — For serious anomalies, raise a concern or audit finding.

Field Verifiers access their submission history from “My Reports” in the sidebar.

6.10 Project Issues & Halting

If problems arise, the RMT can set the project to Halted status. Common reasons include vendor blacklisting, serious compliance issues, unresolved anomaly flags, or extended delays beyond the project timeline. A halted project cannot proceed with installations or claims until the RMT reactivates it.

7. Workflow C — Payment Disbursement

Payment claims are the mechanism by which vendors receive funds for completed milestones. The platform enforces a multi-tier approval chain to ensure all payments are justified by verified results and endorsed by the appropriate oversight bodies.

7.1 Milestone Eligibility

A milestone becomes Claimable when all of its conditions are met. Check milestone eligibility from the project detail page:

  1. Open your project from “Projects Hub”.
  2. Click the Milestones tab to see the milestone plan.
  3. Each milestone card shows its current state: Locked, Pending, Claimable, Claimed, or Paid.
  4. If Claimable, the “Submit Claim” button becomes active.
Note: Typical milestone conditions include: minimum verified installations, minimum female beneficiary percentage, minimum uptime, and no unresolved flags or anomalies.

7.2 Accessing Claims by Role

  • Vendors: “Payment Claims” in sidebar, or Payments tab within a project.
  • RMT: “Disbursements” in sidebar.
  • TAC: “Claim Reviews” in sidebar.
  • PSC: “Disbursements” in sidebar.
  • Auditor: “Claims Audit” in sidebar.

7.3 Submitting a Claim (Vendor)

When a milestone is claimable, follow these steps:

  1. Navigate to the project from “Projects Hub”, click the Payments tab, then “Submit Claim” or “Submit Milestone N Claim”.
  2. Complete the form:
    • Completion Date — When milestone conditions were substantially met.
    • Claim Amount (LSL) — Auto-calculated based on milestone percentage and project budget.
    • Actual Total Beneficiaries — Beneficiaries reached to date.
    • Actual Female Beneficiaries — Female-headed households reached.
    • Implementation Notes — Narrative describing completed work.
  3. Upload Evidence Files — Photos, beneficiary sign-off sheets, progress reports.
  4. Accept Declaration — Confirm all information is accurate.
  5. Click “Submit Claim”.
Warning: Submitting a false or inflated claim may result in payment hold, blacklisting, or legal action. Always ensure your claim accurately reflects verified work.

7.4 Multi-Tier Approval Chain

Once submitted, every claim must pass through four steps:

StepRoleActionStatus After

2 TAC Technical review: audits technical KPIs (energy output, uptime). Click “Endorse Claim” to approve. TAC Endorsed
3 PSC Financial authorization: budget check and compliance review. Click “Approve Payment”. PSC Approved
4 RMT Payment confirmation: open “Confirm Payment” modal, enter transaction reference, click “Submit”. Completed / Paid

7.6 RMT Claim Review Details

From “Disbursements” in the sidebar, open a claim to view full details: milestone conditions, vendor data, KPI data, evidence files, and anomaly flags. Use the review notes textarea and click “RMT Review” to approve forward. Use “Cancel” to close without action.

7.7 TAC Endorsement Details

From “Claim Reviews” in the sidebar, review KPIs including uptime and energy output. Click “Endorse Claim” to approve or “Push Back to RMT” to reject.

7.8 PSC Approval Details

From “Disbursements” in the sidebar, perform budget check and compliance review. Click “Approve Payment” to approve or “Reject Claim” to reject.

7.9 Disbursement Process

After PSC approval, the claim status becomes PSC Approved. The RMT opens the “Confirm Payment” modal, which displays vendor bank details (from their pre-qualification). Enter the transaction/reference ID and click “Submit”. The system updates the milestone to Paid.

7.10 Claim History & Audit Trail

All claim activity is recorded with reviewer name, role, timestamp, and comments. Access the Audit Trail tab from RMT projects for full traceability across the entire payment lifecycle.

8. KPI Dashboard

The KPI dashboard provides real-time performance metrics at project and portfolio levels. Key indicators include:

8.1 Project-Level KPIs

  • Installation Progress — Submitted vs. verified vs. flagged installations against target.
  • Gender KPI — Percentage of female-headed households, vulnerable groups, and low-income beneficiaries.
  • Energy KPI — Monthly kWh generation against targets.
  • Uptime KPI — Average device uptime percentage against target.
  • Milestone Eligibility — Current milestone readiness status.

8.2 Portfolio-Level KPIs

The Portfolio Monitoring view aggregates data across all projects:

  • Total installations target vs. verified
  • Overall gender percentage
  • Average uptime
  • Projects on track, at risk, and completed
  • Total disbursed and pending amounts
  • Comparison against national main program budget

8.3 Accessing KPIs by Role

  • Vendors: Click the KPI tab within any project from “Projects Hub”.
  • RMT: Select “KPI Review” or the KPI tab in project detail.
  • DoE Officers: Use “KPI Review” from the sidebar for regional KPIs.
  • Auditor: Use “KPI Dashboard” from the sidebar.
  • PSC / Donor: Use “Portfolio Overview” for high-level macro KPIs.

9. GIS Installations Map

The GIS map displays all installation sites on an interactive map of Lesotho. Access it from the sidebar via “GIS Mapping” (RMT), “GIS Map” (Auditor), or “Map View” (Field Verifier, Vendor project tab). Each marker is colour-coded by verification status:

  • Green — Verified installation.
  • Yellow — Pending verification.
  • Red — Flagged / anomalous.

Click on a marker to view installation details (beneficiary name, device info, verification status, uptime). The map also includes a summary panel showing total, verified, pending, and flagged counts.

10. Reports Hub

The “Reports” section (sidebar) allows authorised users to generate and download reports in multiple formats (CSV, PDF, Excel). For PSC/Donor users, this is labelled “Briefings & Reports”. Available reports include:

  • Project Progress Reports
  • Installation Summary Reports
  • Payment & Disbursement Reports
  • KPI Performance Reports
  • Audit Trail Reports
  • Vendor Performance Reports

Reports can be generated on demand. Previously generated reports are available in the report history for re-download.

11. Audit & Compliance

11.1 Audit Logs

Every significant action on the platform is logged with timestamps, actor details, old/new values, and module context. The Auditor role can access all logs from “Audit Logs” in the sidebar. Admin users have the same view under “Audit Logs”. RMT users can view project-level audit trails from the Audit Trail tab within any project.

11.2 Anomaly Reports

The “Anomaly Report” section (Auditor sidebar) shows all system-generated anomaly flags. The project-level Anomaly Flags tab (RMT) shows flags per project. The system automatically detects:

  • GPS coordinate duplicates
  • Verification distance threshold violations
  • Data integrity issues

11.3 Audit Findings

The “Audit Findings” section (Auditor sidebar) allows auditors to raise formal findings categorised by type (payment compliance, data integrity, GPS fraud, KPI manipulation, document irregularity, process violation, conflict of interest, etc.) with risk levels (Observation, Minor Finding, Major Finding, Critical).

Findings can be linked to specific projects, claims, installations, or audit log entries. RMT responds to findings, and PSC can provide final comments.

11.4 Issues & Concerns

RMT users can access all issues from “Issues & Findings” in the sidebar. DoE Officers and Auditors can raise concerns about projects. Concerns have types (KPI issue, GPS issue, verification issue, vendor behaviour, etc.), severity levels, and a response thread where RMT can reply and take action. Concerns can be escalated to PSC.

12. Notices & Communications

The “Notice Board” module (RMT and Admin only, accessed from the sidebar) allows publishing notices to all platform users. Notices can be:

  • Categorised (tender, deadline, award, clarification, training, general)
  • Pinned to the top
  • Scheduled for future publication
  • Linked to a specific tender
  • Accompanied by file attachments
  • Set with countdown dates

Email notifications can optionally be sent when a notice is published. The system also supports in-app notifications for events like tender publication, bid submission confirmations, milestone approvals, and field survey submissions.

13. Super Admin Portal

The Super Admin has full access to:

  • “User Management” — Create, edit, activate/deactivate users of any role. Click “Create User” to add a new user.
  • “System Configuration” — Set national program budget, KPI targets (female %, vulnerable %, uptime), anomaly thresholds, GPS duplicate radius, verification max distance, email/SMS settings, file upload limits.
  • “System Health” — Monitor database status, worker queues, scheduler, storage usage, Prospect API connectivity.
  • “Prospect Sync” — View and retry failed synchronisation jobs.
  • “Audit Logs” — Full audit trail of all platform actions.

14. Blacklisting

The blacklisting module manages vendor suspensions and exclusions. The workflow:

  1. Initiation — RMT starts a blacklist case with reason and evidence.
  2. Notice & Cooling-Off — Vendor is notified and given a cooling-off period to respond.
  3. Review — RMT reviews the vendor’s rebuttal (if any).
  4. Confirmation — RMT confirms the blacklisting (temporary with expiry or permanent).
  5. Appeal — Vendor may appeal; RMT reviews the appeal.
  6. Reinstatement — If the blacklist expires or the appeal is upheld, the vendor is reinstated.
Warning: Blacklisted vendors cannot bid on tenders or receive payments. Their existing projects enter a halted state.

15. Prospect Sync

The platform synchronises data with Prospect, an external M&E (Monitoring & Evaluation) system. The following entities are synced:

  • Agents — Vendor profiles are pushed as Prospect agents after setup completion.
  • Customers — Beneficiaries / installation records.
  • Installations — Installation reports are queued for sync after submission.
  • Targets — Project targets are pushed after project creation and on update.
  • Reports — Monthly report data.

Sync logs are visible in the Super Admin Portal. Failed jobs can be retried via the “Refresh Panel” button or using the management command: 

python manage.py retry_prospect_syncs

16. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Why can't I see any tenders?

Ensure your vendor account is pre-qualified and approved. Only approved vendors can see published tenders in “Published Tenders”. Also check that your account status is “Active” and not “Suspended” or “Blacklisted.”

Q: My bid was rejected. Can I resubmit?

If the RMT requests revisions, you can resubmit. Otherwise, you may need to wait for the next tender cycle.

Q: How do I update my company information?

Navigate to “My Profile” in the sidebar. The profile has tabs: Company Profile, Documents, Bids & Tenders, Projects, Performance, Payments, and Audit Trail. Click “Edit Profile” to update contact details, bank information, and uploaded documents. Changes to pre-qualification status require re-review by the RMT.

Q: When will I receive payment after submitting a claim?

Payment processing time depends on the multi-tier approval workflow (RMT → TAC → PSC). Each tier must approve before disbursement is initiated. Check the claim status from “Payment Claims” in the sidebar or the Payments tab within your project.

Q: What does the “Flagged” status mean on my installation?

Flagged installations require attention. A flag is raised when GPS coordinates are duplicated, verification distance exceeds thresholds, or discrepancies are found. The RMT will review and either resolve or escalate the flag.

Q: How do I use the GIS map?

The GIS map is available from the sidebar (“GIS Mapping” for RMT, “GIS Map” for Auditor, “Map View” for Field Verifier). It loads all installation locations in Lesotho. Use mouse scroll to zoom, click markers to view details.

Q: Can I download reports?

Yes, the “Reports” section (or “Briefings & Reports” for PSC) allows downloading in CSV, PDF, and Excel formats. Available reports depend on your role and permissions.
